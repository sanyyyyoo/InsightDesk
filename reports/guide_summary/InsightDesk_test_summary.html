InsightDesk - summary of tests for the paper

Support-complaint intelligence: raw complaints are grouped into problem cases, then spikes, cause hypotheses and escalation are computed per case. Every test below had its pass criteria written before it ran. P = precision (share of merges that are correct), R = recall (share of true duplicates found), F1 = their balance; "case P" = how pure each case is.

6. Questions reviewers are likely to ask (and our answer)

  • Why not just use ChatGPT/Claude? They group well on small offline batches but change groups with input order (78-95% of grouped pairs survive a reshuffle, vs 99% for an identical rerun) and cannot hold a company's full history; counting and alerts need stable cases. As labellers they do well only with our codebook, which was written from the test reviews; with a problem list they build themselves, precision drops to 0.62 (ours 0.81).
  • Recall is low on reviews (0.33). Short reviews (21 words) look no more alike than unrelated ones; even people agree only at kappa 0.57-0.74. Re-joining recovers +26%; alert groups and the outage alarm keep incident detection high despite split cases.
  • Labels made by the team? Benchmarks use independent Bugzilla labels (P 0.87-0.96); Play Store labels have documented agreement and a blind relabel round.
  • Similar to Oracle 2026 (retrieve-then-verify)? Cited openly; we add online cases, re-joining, per-case incident signals and the first labelled evaluation of this design.
